# BARVA — Tips and Hints

These tips are designed to help without removing the pleasure of discovering the simulation for yourself.

---

## Getting Through the Opening Nights

### 1. Survive before you specialise

The early game is not the moment to build the greatest pub in Britain. First prove that the doors can open, the customers can be served and the bills can be paid.

### 2. Start with moderate prices

A sensible early price range protects both volume and margin. The slider can move from **x0.50** to **x2.50**, but the edges are tools, not a lifestyle.

### 3. Change one major thing at a time

Hiring staff, changing prices, launching an event and buying an upgrade on the same night makes the result impossible to read. Run controlled experiments.

### 4. Keep Payday money separate in your head

The cash shown on screen is not all available spending money. Wages, rent and invoices already have claims on part of it.

### 5. Build a two-to-three-week reserve

Once the pub becomes stable, aim to hold enough cash to survive several weak nights or an unexpected incident. Reserves turn disasters into inconveniences.

---

## Service and Staffing

### 6. Check workload before blaming staff quality

If workload is far above 1.0, even good staff will look poor. Add capacity or reduce demand before replacing everyone.

### 7. The healthy workload zone is roughly 0.8–1.2

Below this range you have spare capacity. Above it, pressure begins to damage service and stability. Sustained workload above **1.8** is a genuine emergency.

### 8. Aim for 60+ in key driver metrics

Average Speed, Quality, Composure and Reliability become much safer once they reach the 60 range.

### 9. Quality prevents refunds

Fast service is useful, but speed without quality can still damage reputation and create refunds.

### 10. Morale drives reliability

When reliability falls, inspect morale and wage history. The problem may not be the staff member’s base ability—it may be the conditions you created.

### 11. Hire slightly before a planned surge

Do not wait until a large event has already overwhelmed the bar. Staffing is cheaper than repairing a chaos spiral.

### 12. Never casually miss wages

One missed payment can trigger morale loss, misconduct and departures. The resulting service collapse may cost far more than the original wage bill.

---

## Stock and Money

### 13. Stockouts and overstock are both expensive

A stockout loses immediate sales and reputation. Overstock traps cash and creates waste. Use recent demand rather than optimism.

### 14. Event nights require more than event stock

Extra customers also create more service pressure, security risk and potential food demand. Prepare the whole operation.

### 15. Trade credit is still debt

Supplier credit can solve a timing problem. It cannot make an unprofitable order profitable.

### 16. Borrow to cross a gap, not to avoid a decision

Credit is useful when income and bills arrive at different times. It becomes dangerous when used repeatedly to cover the same operating loss.

### 17. Pay down expensive debt during strong weeks

A good week is not permission to create a larger permanent cost base. Use part of the surplus to remove future pressure.

---

## Security and Chaos

### 18. Invest in security before it feels urgent

Once chaos is already high, security is trying to stop a fire rather than prevent one.

### 19. Trading Standards warnings outrank almost everything

Underage service violations can escalate to severe reputation damage, a **£300** fine and licence loss at **9+ strikes**. Two strikes is already serious. Tighten the door immediately when warnings rise.

### 20. Big crowds change the correct security policy

**Friendly Welcome** may work on a quiet room. **Balanced Door** or **Strict Door** belongs on packed, rowdy or unfamiliar nights.

### 21. Chaos attacks morale

High chaos does not end when the incident ends. It leaves the staff more stressed and less reliable for later rounds.

### 22. Break a chaos spiral by reducing pressure

Tighten security, cancel unnecessary demand boosts, protect morale and accept a quieter night. Pride is not a recognised payment method.

---

## Activities, Identity and Reputation

### 23. Activities are amplifiers

A strong team turns an activity into growth. A weak team turns it into evidence.

### 24. Align events with the pub’s direction

Repeated social, cultural, food or high-energy events help form identity. Aligned choices receive stronger long-term benefits than random variety.

### 25. Do not chase every milestone immediately

Milestones are guidance, not a shopping list. Pursue the ones that fit the business’s current strengths and weaknesses.

### 26. Reputation supports pricing power

Raise prices because the venue has earned it—not because the rent is due.

### 27. One bad rumour is not a rebrand

Correct the cause, then produce consistent good nights. Public perception often lags behind reality.

### 28. VIP loyalty is built through consistency

Learn what each important regular values. A venue that naturally matches them is easier to maintain than constant special treatment.

---

## Upgrades and Expansion

### 29. Fix the bottleneck you actually have

More stock capacity does not fix slow service. Better entertainment does not fix security. Upgrade the part of the system limiting results now.

### 30. Permanent costs deserve permanent value

Expansion may increase revenue but often adds wages, maintenance and complexity. Judge the weekly effect, not only the exciting new feature.

### 31. Delay the inn until the pub is boringly stable

The inn is a second operation. If the main pub still needs heroic intervention every night, adding rooms is not expansion—it is multiplying the emergency.

### 32. Leave room for the installation cost and the next Payday

Do not spend the final pound on an upgrade and then discover the staff would also quite like paying.

---

## Reading Reports

### 33. Night reports are tactical

Use them to identify immediate service, stock, security and morale issues.

### 34. Weekly reports are strategic

Use them to judge cash flow, reputation direction, staffing stability and whether your current plan is working.

### 35. Longer trends reveal identity and structural problems

A single night contains noise. Several weeks reveal the business.

### 36. Follow causes, not symptoms

Examples:

- Unserved customers may be caused by workload.
- High workload may be caused by an event or weak staffing.
- Weak staffing may be caused by low morale.
- Low morale may be caused by missed wages or chaos.

Fixing only the final symptom guarantees a sequel.

## Emergency Playbooks

### Cash Emergency

1. Cancel non-essential spending.
2. Stop speculative upgrades.
3. Reduce wasteful orders.
4. Check whether prices are too low or service capacity is wasting demand.
5. Use credit only if the corrected operation can repay it.

### Service Emergency

1. Check workload.
2. Add staff capacity or reduce demand.
3. Use a temporary Landlord Action.
4. Avoid another large event.
5. Improve quality only after capacity is under control.

### Morale Emergency

1. Pay all essential wages.
2. Reduce chaos and workload.
3. Use morale-supporting actions or management.
4. Avoid forcing another packed night.
5. Rebuild through several calm services.

### Trading Standards Emergency

1. Switch from Friendly Welcome to Balanced Door or Strict Door.
2. Improve bouncers, base security level and door policy.
3. Avoid unmanaged high-volume events.
4. Stop abusing Happy Hour if it is creating Fair Trading strikes.
5. Treat closure risk as more important than short-term profit.

## Advanced Hints

### 37. Strong identity can outperform isolated “optimal” choices

A coherent Respectable, Artsy, Underground, Family-Friendly, Shady or Rowdy venue gains compounding benefits across customers, activities, reputation and actions.

### 38. Better customers reduce several problems at once

Improved reputation can attract customers with more money and lower trouble risk. This can raise revenue while reducing chaos—a powerful positive loop.

### 39. Calm nights can be an investment

A lower-revenue night that restores morale, reliability and reputation may produce more value than another overloaded event.

### 40. Resilience beats perfection

The best-run pub is not the one that never suffers an incident. It is the one that can absorb the incident without entering a death spiral.

---

## Ten Things the Game Quietly Tests

1. Whether you understand cash flow rather than cash balance.
2. Whether you can distinguish demand from useful demand.
3. Whether you prepare before creating pressure.
4. Whether you protect the team that produces the revenue.
5. Whether you recognise a feedback loop early.
6. Whether you use debt deliberately.
7. Whether you build a coherent identity.
8. Whether you read long-term trends.
9. Whether you can delay expansion.
10. Whether you know when a quiet night is the winning move.
