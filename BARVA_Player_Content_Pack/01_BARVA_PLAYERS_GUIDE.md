# BARVA — Player’s Guide

## Welcome to the Night Shift

In **BARVA**, you are not simply serving drinks. You are running a living pub where money, staff, customers, reputation, security and atmosphere constantly affect one another.

A packed house can make your week—or expose every weakness in the business.

Your job is to survive the early nights, build a dependable operation, develop a clear identity and turn a struggling local into a venue people remember.

---

## 1. The Aim of the Game

BARVA does not end with one fixed victory screen. Progress is measured by the strength and reputation of the pub you build.

A successful landlord will:

- maintain positive cash flow;
- pay wages, rent and suppliers on time;
- keep stock available without wasting money;
- build a reliable and motivated team;
- control chaos, crime and regulatory risk;
- improve the pub through upgrades;
- unlock activities and new opportunities;
- develop loyal regulars and a recognisable identity;
- survive long enough to pursue prestige.

You can fail by exhausting your money and credit, repeatedly missing essential payments, losing your workforce, destroying your reputation or allowing Trading Standards to revoke the licence at **9+ strikes**.

---

## 2. The Core Game Loop

BARVA is played through a repeating cycle of **preparation, service, review and payday**.

### Before Opening

Use the preparation phase to decide what kind of night you are about to run.

Check:

- available drink and food stock;
- staff coverage and morale;
- expected customer demand;
- your current pricing;
- security policy;
- scheduled activities;
- upcoming bills and available cash.

A quiet normal night requires less preparation than a promoted event. Never create more demand than your staff, stock and security can handle.

### During Service

Open the pub and advance through the night one round at a time.

During each round:

- customers arrive and attempt to buy;
- staff use their available capacity to serve them;
- unserved customers become frustrated;
- trouble, refunds, theft and other incidents may occur;
- chaos and morale may change;
- scheduled activities and active effects continue;
- you may use available Landlord Actions to intervene.

Watch the live information rather than advancing blindly. One overloaded round can become several bad rounds if the underlying problem is ignored.

### Closing the Night

When service ends, review the Night Report.

Pay attention to:

- revenue and operating costs;
- customers served and left unserved;
- refunds and stockouts;
- fights, theft and other incidents;
- reputation change;
- staff morale movement;
- service and stability drivers;
- emerging rumours or warnings.

The report is not just a scorecard. It tells you what to change before the next opening.

### Payday

Every seven nights, the week closes and Payday begins.

This is when the business must meet its obligations, including:

- staff wages;
- rent;
- supplier invoices;
- credit repayments and interest;
- other debts or penalties.

A profitable-looking pub can still fail at Payday if too much cash has been tied up in stock, upgrades or expansion. Keep a reserve.

---

## 3. Reading the Main Resources

### Cash

Cash keeps the doors open. It is used for stock, wages, rent, activities, upgrades and emergency costs.

Do not judge the business only by tonight’s profit. Always consider what must be paid at the end of the week.

### Reputation

Reputation affects demand, customer quality and how much people are prepared to pay.

Good service, calm nights, successful activities and positive stories improve it. Fights, refunds, poor service and broken promises damage it.

Reputation moves more slowly than a single night. Patterns matter more than one lucky or unlucky result.

### Chaos

Chaos represents instability inside the venue.

Low chaos means the pub is controlled. Rising chaos increases the risk of fights, theft, staff stress and a wider operational collapse.

As a practical guide:

| Chaos | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0–15 | Calm |
| 16–35 | Normal |
| 36–59 | Elevated |
| 60–79 | High risk |
| 80–100 | Critical |

### Staff Morale

Morale affects reliability, behaviour and retention.

| Morale | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 80+ | Excellent |
| 65–79 | Healthy |
| 50–64 | Unsettled |
| 40–49 | Poor |
| Below 40 | Critical |

Low morale is rarely an isolated problem. It often leads to weaker service, misconduct, departures and even greater pressure on the staff who remain.

### Inventory

Stock creates revenue, but excess stock traps cash and may spoil.

Order enough to meet realistic demand. Large events, seasonal surges and premium customers may require more stock or a different product mix.

### Trading Standards Violations

Underage service violations are one of the most dangerous threats in the game.

Violations accumulate during the week and reset when the new week begins, but penalties trigger as the strike count rises. At **2–4 strikes**, the pub takes a heavy reputation hit. At **5–8 strikes**, the hit is worse and a **£300** fine lands. At **9+ strikes**, the licence is revoked immediately.

Serving underage punters is the main danger. Happy Hour can also create Fair Trading strikes when a sale lands in the **75–125% base-price band**. Base security, bouncer quality, **Balanced Door** and **Strict Door** reduce the underage-service risk. Treat early warnings seriously.

---

## 4. Customers

Customers—often called **punters**—arrive with different budgets, expectations and levels of trouble.

### Customer Tiers

| Tier | Typical Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Lowlife | £3–25 wallet, highest trouble risk |
| Regular | £8–58 wallet, dependable core crowd |
| Decent | £18–108 wallet, better spending and lower trouble risk |
| Big Spender | £35–175 wallet, high spending power with the lowest trouble risk |

Customers who are served well may remain, spend again and improve the pub’s story. Customers left waiting can become rowdy, turn into a menace or leave angry.

Higher reputation generally attracts a stronger customer mix. Premium prices work best when the venue, service and crowd justify them.

---

## 5. Staffing the Pub

The team is the engine of the business.

### Main Roles

- **Bartenders** provide the core drink-service capacity, from trainees to speed, charisma and security specialists.
- **Kitchen staff** prepare food once the kitchen is operating.
- **Security staff and bouncers** reduce incidents and improve ID checking.
- **Managers** improve efficiency and support morale.
- **Reception and housekeeping staff** cover inn bookings and rooms once lodging is unlocked.
- **Duty Managers and Marshalls** help control inn operations; Marshalls are hired from the security side after the Marshalls upgrade.

Staff differ in speed, skill, quality, composure and reliability. The cheapest hire is not always the cheapest outcome.

### Workload

Workload compares customer demand with staff capacity.

- Below roughly **0.8**: spare capacity;
- around **0.8–1.2**: healthy operating range;
- above **1.2**: service pressure begins to rise;
- above **1.8**: severe overload and cascading failure risk.

High workload harms more than serving speed. It can increase refunds, chaos, staff misconduct and morale loss.

### Driver Metrics

Night reports may show Service and Stability Drivers.

- **Average Speed** measures how quickly the team can serve.
- **Quality** affects satisfaction and refunds.
- **Composure** limits chaos under pressure.
- **Reliability** reduces misconduct and instability.

Aim for performance values of **60 or higher**, while keeping workload near the balanced range.

---

## 6. Stock, Suppliers and Pricing

### Ordering Stock

Use expected demand, recent sales and upcoming activities to decide how much to buy.

Avoid two extremes:

- **too little stock**, which creates lost sales and angry customers;
- **too much stock**, which wastes cash and increases spoilage.

Supplier trust and bulk-order options improve over time. Trade credit can help with timing, but the invoice will still arrive at Payday.

### Setting Prices

The main price slider runs from **x0.50** to **x2.50**. Happy Hour halves the effective drink price while it is active.

- Lower prices improve affordability but reduce margin.
- Higher prices improve margin but can reduce demand and increase complaints.
- Strong reputation and premium customers support higher prices.
- Value-focused regulars and VIPs dislike aggressive pricing.

For a developing pub, moderate pricing is normally safer than chasing maximum margin from the first night.

---

## 7. Security and Compliance

Security protects more than the furniture.

It reduces:

- fights;
- theft;
- break-ins;
- chaos;
- morale damage;
- underage service violations.

Security includes three door policies—**Friendly Welcome**, **Balanced Door** and **Strict Door**—plus bouncers, Marshalls, CCTV, lighting, reinforced doors, burglar alarms, door-team upgrades and timed security tasks.

Use **Balanced Door** or **Strict Door** when the pub is expecting a large, rowdy or unfamiliar crowd. **Friendly Welcome** keeps traffic warmer, but it also increases exposure.

Security is most effective when purchased before the crisis. Buying the lock after the glass is already on the floor is poor timing.

---

## 8. Activities and Events

Activities can increase traffic, revenue and identity.

Examples include:

- quiz nights;
- karaoke;
- open-mic sessions;
- live acoustic sets, live bands and DJs;
- darts competitions and pool nights;
- sports nights and local sports screenings;
- food service nights, themed food, chef’s specials and tasting menus;
- charity events;
- family lunches;
- cocktail promos, ladies nights and brewery takeovers.

Every activity has a cost and creates additional demand. It may also attract a particular kind of customer and push the pub toward a particular identity.

Before scheduling an activity, ask:

1. Do I have enough stock?
2. Can the staff handle the extra workload?
3. Is security strong enough?
4. Does the event fit the pub I am building?
5. Can I absorb the cost if the night underperforms?

An activity is a force multiplier. It multiplies strengths—but it is equally talented at multiplying weaknesses.

---

## 9. Landlord Actions

Landlord Actions are limited tactical interventions used during service.

They may let you:

- work the room;
- run a special;
- push an upsell;
- host locals, partner or charity promotions;
- support VIPs and reviewers;
- shape the door policy;
- plant or control a story;
- take riskier moves against rivals.

Actions may cost money, take time to resolve and enter cooldown before they can be used again.

Do not spend them automatically. Save them for moments when a small intervention can prevent a larger collapse.

---

## 10. Reputation, Rumours and Pub Identity

### Rumours

Stories about the pub can spread through customers or staff.

Rumours may concern:

- service;
- prices;
- safety;
- theft;
- food quality;
- favouritism;
- staff behaviour.

A rumour can affect perception before the full truth is visible. Consistently good operations eventually correct false negativity; consistently poor operations eventually expose empty hype.

### Identity

The game watches the decisions you repeat. Over time the pub may become known as:

- Rowdy;
- Respectable;
- Artsy;
- Shady;
- Underground;
- Family-Friendly;
- or settle back to Neutral.

Identity affects the customers you attract and increases the value of aligned activities and actions.

A clear strategy compounds. Randomly changing direction every night makes the business harder to understand and harder to optimise.

---

## 11. VIP Regulars

Some returning customers may become named VIPs with persistent loyalty.

Different VIPs value different things, such as:

- good service;
- fair prices;
- calm nights;
- quality food;
- regular activities.

Highly loyal VIPs can become advocates who boost demand and protect the pub’s reputation. Neglected VIPs can turn against the venue and generate damaging stories.

You do not need to satisfy every VIP in exactly the same way. Build a pub whose strengths naturally suit the regulars you want to keep.

---

## 12. Milestones, Pub Levels and Upgrades

The game currently has **nineteen** milestones. They reward strong play and unlock new activities, upgrades, supplier bulk tiers and Landlord Action tiers.

They cover achievements such as:

- surviving the opening nights;
- avoiding stockouts;
- delivering perfect service;
- paying essential bills on time;
- restoring order after chaos;
- building a recognisable identity;
- using debt responsibly;
- producing sustained profitable weeks.

Pub levels require both achievement and time. You cannot rush the entire progression system in one explosive week.

### Choosing Upgrades

Upgrades can improve:

- bar and cellar capacity;
- kitchen capability;
- security;
- staff morale;
- entertainment;
- room capacity.

Buy upgrades that solve a real bottleneck.

A larger cellar is useful when stock capacity is limiting sales. It is dead money when the true problem is that nobody is being served.

---

## 13. The Inn

Later progression may unlock lodging as a second business.

Inn Wings add room capacity in tiers: **3, 6, 10, 15 and 25 rooms**. Rooms create new income but also introduce:

- cleaning and maintenance;
- separate pricing;
- guest incidents;
- room reputation;
- additional staffing pressure.

Room price starts at **£45**, and sharp price swings during service can hurt inn reputation and add instability. The inn can stabilise a healthy pub. It can also sink an unstable one faster.

Expand only when the main venue can operate without constant emergency intervention.

---

## 14. Seasons and Rivals

Seasonal conditions alter demand and customer behaviour. **Tourist Wave**, **Exam Season**, **Winter Slump** and **Derby Week** may change the crowd, stock needs and level of risk.

Rival venues may also create weekly pressure through **Price War**, **Quality Push**, **Event Spam**, **Lay Low** or **Chaos Recovery** stances.

Do not copy every competitor move. Understand whether the pressure is temporary, then respond in a way that still fits your own strategy.

---

## 15. A Safe First-Week Plan

### Nights 1–2

- Keep pricing moderate.
- Learn the relationship between demand and service capacity.
- Avoid expensive activities.
- Watch stock usage and customer frustration.

### Nights 3–4

- Correct any obvious staffing bottleneck.
- Protect morale.
- Begin building a cash reserve.
- Review security and compliance warnings.

### Nights 5–6

- Test one controlled improvement, such as a small activity, targeted hire or useful upgrade.
- Do not make several large changes at once; you need to know what caused the result.

### Night 7 and Payday

- Hold enough cash for wages, rent and invoices.
- Review the entire week rather than obsessing over the best or worst night.
- Choose one operational weakness to fix next week.

---

## 16. Recovery Guide

### When Cash Is Collapsing

- stop non-essential upgrades and activities;
- reduce waste and over-ordering;
- inspect pricing and service throughput;
- use credit only as a bridge;
- fix the structural loss before borrowing again.

### When Chaos Is Rising

- tighten security policy;
- add or improve security staff;
- reduce event pressure;
- use calming Landlord Actions;
- protect morale before staff reliability collapses.

### When Service Is Failing

- inspect workload first;
- add capacity or reduce demand;
- improve staff quality if workload is already healthy;
- avoid high-volume events until the team stabilises.

### When Reputation Is Falling

- remove the operational cause;
- produce several clean, consistent nights;
- resist wild nightly strategy changes;
- use reports to distinguish a temporary rumour from a real quality problem.

---

## 17. The Golden Rules

1. **Cash is oxygen.**
2. **Payday matters more than tonight’s headline profit.**
3. **Workload reveals whether the team can actually cope.**
4. **Security is prevention, not decoration.**
5. **Morale problems become service problems.**
6. **Activities expose weak operations.**
7. **Credit should bridge timing—not disguise failure.**
8. **Buy upgrades for today’s bottleneck.**
9. **Build a recognisable pub rather than chasing every bonus.**
10. **Read the reports. The game is telling you why you are losing.**

The doors are open. Make the night count.
